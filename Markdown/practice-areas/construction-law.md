# Construction law and litigation

[Home](../README.md) · [Matter core](../modules/matter-management.md) · [Evidence](../modules/documents-and-evidence.md)

Model project/property, owner, developer, general contractor, subcontractors, suppliers, design professionals, sureties, insurers, contracts, change orders, payment applications, notices, claimed defects, inspections, photos, schedules, estimates, expert reports, and damages. Parties are related entities with matter roles; one organization may have different roles across matters.

Keep contract amount, change-order amount, claimed damages, and settlement amount distinct. Link each allegation/damage item to evidence and provenance. Track disputed facts rather than storing every allegation as accepted truth.

Typical configurable phases: conflict review, engagement, project/contract review, evidence preservation, notices, pre-suit negotiation, pleadings, discovery, experts, mediation, trial/arbitration, judgment/enforcement, resolution/closure. Actual paths depend on engagement and forum.

Lien/bond/notice/limitations proposals require jurisdiction, project type, role, contract/delivery facts, governing source, and attorney verification. Do not hard-code universal deadlines or promise automatic lien validity. Preserve service/notice evidence and delivery status separately from task completion.

The client portal emphasizes requested contracts/photos, approved project chronology, next appointment, reviewed status, and outstanding decisions. Staff retain internal expert analysis, privileged strategy, opposing-party records, and drafts behind appropriate access policies.

Pilot acceptance uses a sanitized construction dispute with multiple contract parties, a changed trigger date, preserved evidence, one reviewed deadline, and one client update. The firm confirms templates against its actual practice before production.
